# Abstract 

Transformer architectures have achieved remark-able empirical success in modeling contextual relationships in natural language, yet a precise mathematical characterization of their expressive power remains incomplete. In this work, we in-troduce a measure-theoretic framework for con-textual representations in which texts are mod-eled as probability measures over a semantic em-bedding space, and contextual relations between words, are represented as coupling measures be-tween them. Within this setting, we introduce 

Sinkhorn Transformers , a transformer-like archi-tecture. Our main result is a universal approxima-tion theorem: any continuous coupling function between probability measures, that encodes the semantic relation coupling measure, can be uni-formly approximated by a Sinkhorn Transformer with appropriate parameters. 

# 1. Introduction

Despite this success, a precise mathematical char-acterization of the class of contextual relations representable by attention mechanisms remains incomplete. Existing anal-yses typically describe attention as a heuristic weighting scheme over representations, leaving open the question of what functional objects attention layers are fundamentally capable of approximating. In this work, we study whether Transformers can learn every possible system of semantic contextual relations. # 2. Related Work 

Expressivity and universality of attention mechanisms. 

The expressive power of attention-based architectures has been studied from multiple perspectives, including univer-sality results for sequence-to-sequence models and analyses of architectural limitations (Vaswani et al., 2017; Yun et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2021; P ´erez et al., 2021; Furuya et al., 2024). These works typically model attention as a weighted aggregation operator acting on finite-dimensional represen-tations, and establish universal approximation results for functions with codomain Rd. For instance, (Yun et al., 2020; Furuya et al., 2024) prove universality for classes of functions of the form P (X) × X → Rd or sequence-to-sequence maps with vector-valued outputs. In contrast, our work studies attention at the level of the contextual relations it induces , modeling attention mechanisms as op-erators that assign joint probability measures to pairs of input distributions. This shift from vector-valued outputs to coupling-valued outputs enables a characterization of ex-pressivity in terms of approximation of structured relations, rather than pointwise representations in Rd.

Optimal transport in attention and representation learn-ing. Optimal transport and entropic regularization have been widely adopted in machine learning as tools for com-paring distributions and inducing geometry-aware similar-ities (Cuturi, 2013; Genevay et al., 2016; Peyr ´e & Cuturi, 2019). Several works incorporate Sinkhorn iterations into attention mechanisms to improve stability or to encode prior structure (Frogner et al., 2015; Mena et al., 2018). In these approaches, optimal transport is typically used as a computational primitive or loss function acting on learned representations. By contrast, we take coupling measures themselves as the objects of approximation. This allows us to treat Sinkhorn-based attention as a parametrized fam-ily of coupling-valued maps and to study their expressive power within a functional-analytic and measure-theoretic framework (L ´eonard, 2012; Nutz & Wiesel, 2022b;a). (Tay et al., 2020a) propose replacing standard softmax atten-tion with Sinkhorn normalization at every layer, enforcing (approximate) double stochasticity throughout the network. Similarly, (Tay et al., 2020b) introduce Sparse Sinkhorn Attention, combining Sinkhorn iterations with sparsity con-straints to obtain more efficient attention patterns. Our approach differs in both scope and objective. Rather than modifying all attention layers, we introduce the Sinkhorn operator only at the final interaction stage, keep-ing standard attention mechanisms in intermediate layers. This allows us to retain the expressive structure of classical Transformers while introducing a principled probabilistic in-terpretation of the final attention map as a coupling between measures. Moreover, while prior works are primarily motivated by architectural design and computational efficiency, our focus is on expressivity and approximation theory. In particular, we study the ability of Transformer-like architectures to approximate structured relational operators (i.e., couplings between measures), providing a theoretical characterization of contextual relation learning. 

Semantic interpretation of attention mechanisms. At-tention mechanisms are often heuristically interpreted as capturing semantic similarity or contextual relevance in lan-guage models. Such interpretations, however, typically lack a precise mathematical formulation of what it means for a model to represent semantic relations. Our work differs by providing a formal, measure-theoretic interpretation of con-textual understanding, in which semantic relations are mod-eled as coupling measures between semantic distributions. Within this framework, our universal approximation result yields a precise characterization of the class of semantic relations that attention mechanisms can represent, going be-yond vector-valued similarity scores and embedding-based interpretations.

# 4. The Space of Coupling systems 

We now study the functional-analytic structure of semantic coupling functions. Our goal is to characterize the space in which these objects live and to identify natural regular-ity assumptions under which approximation results can be established. We consider the space of continuous functions: 

C = C(P(X) × P (Y ), M(X × Y )) ,

where M(X × Y ) denotes the space of signed Radon mea-sures on X × Y equipped with the weak* topology (i.e., the topology induced by the dual pairing with C(X × Y )). We endow C with the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets, which in this case is equivalent to uniform convergence on the entire space since P(X) × P (Y ) is compact. 6.1. Practical Implications and Relation to Standard Transformers 

In practical applications, the proposed architecture admits a direct implementation. In the discrete setting, texts cor-respond to uniform empirical measures (as discussed in Section 3.3), and Transformer encoders are implemented in the standard way. The Sinkhorn operator can be efficiently computed using the Sinkhorn algorithm, resulting in an ar-chitecture that closely matches a standard Transformer with a final Sinkhorn normalization applied to the score matrix 

⟨Qi, K j ⟩.Regarding the processing of inputs, we distinguish two cases. In the single-text setting (i.e., self-attention), both inputs co-incide, and the architecture processes the same text through the encoders. This corresponds to a standard Transformer without cross connection between attention heads and MLP of each branch. As illustrated in Figure 4, the architecture follows a classical part of the Transformer architecture, with the Sinkhorn operator applied as a final layer.  

> Figure 4. Sinkhorn Transformer architecture in the single-text setting.

In the two-text setting, the architecture follows the standard encoder-decoder paradigm: each text is first processed inde-pendently, and the interaction between them is introduced at the final stage through the Sinkhorn layer. This plays a role analogous to cross-attention. Figure 5 illustrates the corre-sponding structure and highlights the part of the standard Transformer architecture that is preserved. We now discuss the role of the Sinkhorn operator compared to the classical softmax attention. The softmax operation produces row-stochastic matrices, corresponding to condi-tional probability distributions. In contrast, the Sinkhorn operator produces approximately doubly stochastic matrices,  

> Figure 5. Sinkhorn Transformer architecture in the two-text setting.

enforcing both row and column normalization. This addi-tional structure can be interpreted as promoting balanced interactions between tokens. Importantly, empirical evidence (e.g., (Tay et al., 2020a)) suggests that attention matrices in trained models are often close to doubly stochastic. The Sinkhorn operator can be efficiently implemented as an iterative normalization proce-dure, typically converging in a small number of iterations, and is fully differentiable. In this sense, our architecture remains very close to standard Transformers: it preserves classical attention mechanisms in all intermediate layers and modifies only the final nor-malization step. This is in contrast with approaches such as (Tay et al., 2020a), which replace all softmax layers with Sinkhorn normalization. This shows that the proposed framework does not depart from standard Transformer ar-chitectures, but rather provides a structured reinterpretation of their final interaction mechanism.

# 8. Conclusion and Future Work 

We introduced a measure-theoretic framework for contex-tual representations in which attention mechanisms are mod-eled as operators that assign coupling measures between se-mantic distributions. Within this framework, we established a universal approximation result characterizing the expres-sive power of Transformer-style attention mechanisms at the level of contextual relations. Beyond the formal result, our analysis provides a principled interpretation of semantic representation in attention-based models. Rather than treating attention weights as heuristic similarity scores, our framework views semantic understand-ing as the ability to represent structured probabilistic rela-tions between meanings, formalized as coupling measures with prescribed marginals. Our work focuses on representational expressivity and does not address questions of learning dynamics, statistical effi-ciency, or generalization. Understanding how such coupling-valued representations can be learned from finite data, and under what conditions they arise in practice, remains an open problem. Several directions for future work naturally emerge from our results. First, it would be of interest to derive quantita-tive approximation rates and sample complexity bounds for coupling-valued attention mechanisms. Second, extending the present framework to sequential or dynamical settings could provide insight into the role of attention in autoregres-sive and generative models. Finally, exploring alternative regularization schemes beyond entropic optimal transport may lead to richer classes of contextual relations while pre-serving analytical tractability. 

# 9. Impact Statement 

This paper presents theoretical work whose primary goal is to advance the mathematical understanding of Transformer architectures and attention mechanisms. In particular, it develops a rigorous framework connecting contextual at-tention with optimal transport and establishes a universal approximation result for semantic coupling functions. The results are foundational in nature and do not introduce new models, training procedures, datasets, or deployment strate-gies. As such, the work does not pose direct ethical risks nor immediate societal consequences beyond those already well established in the broader field of machine learning. More broadly, improved theoretical understanding of widely used architectures may contribute in the long term to the development of more interpretable, reliable, and principled learning systems. Any potential societal impact of this work is therefore indirect and mediated by future applications and deployments, which fall outside the scope of the present paper.
