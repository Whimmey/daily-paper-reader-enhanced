Abstract 

Hybrid language models that combine attention mechanisms with alterna-tive sequence-processing components—such as state space models (SSMs) or linear attention—have achieved competitive performance while offering im-proved efficiency at inference time. However, it remains unclear whether these alternative components are genuinely utilized by the trained models or effectively bypassed in favor of the attention pathway. We present a systematic functional component ablation framework applied to two archi-tecturally distinct sub-1B hybrid models: Qwen3.5-0.8B , which interleaves Gated DeltaNet linear attention and full softmax attention layers sequen-tially, and Falcon-H1-0.5B , which processes both Mamba-2 SSM and at-tention pathways in parallel within each block, with a pure Transformer control ( Qwen2.5-0.5B ). Through exhaustive group ablations, layer-wise sweeps across 36 layers (Falcon) and 24 layers (Qwen), positional ablations, matched random controls, perplexity analysis, and hidden-state contribution metrics, we establish four main findings: (1) both component types are essen-tial and neither is bypassed; (2) the alternative component (linear attention in Qwen, SSM in Falcon) serves as the primary language modeling backbone ,causing catastrophic perplexity degradation ( >35,000 ×) when removed ver-

1. Introduction 

The Transformer architecture [1] has been the dominant paradigm for large language models (LLMs), with softmax self-attention serving as its core sequence-mixing mechanism. However, the quadratic computational complexity of attention with respect to sequence length has motivated the development of alternative approaches. State space models (SSMs) [2], par-ticularly their selective variants such as Mamba [3] and Mamba-2 [4], offer linear-time alternatives with competitive performance. Similarly, linear at-tention mechanisms based on gated recurrences [5] provide efficient alterna-tives to softmax attention while maintaining expressiveness through data-dependent gating. Rather than choosing between attention and these alternatives, recent work has converged on hybrid architectures that combine both. The Falcon-H1 family [6] employs a parallel hybrid design where each block processes its input through both a Mamba-2 SSM and an attention head simultaneously, summing their outputs. Qwen3.5 [7] adopts a sequential hybrid approach, interleaving dedicated linear attention layers (based on Gated DeltaNet) with full softmax attention layers throughout the network depth. A critical question arises: do hybrid models actually use both com-ponents, or does one pathway dominate while the other is effec-tively bypassed? Recent work by Benfeghoul et al. [8] found that in post-training linearization methods, the linear attention component is often inad-vertently bypassed. Wang et al. [9] showed that the choice of hybridization 2ratio significantly affects recall performance, suggesting that the interplay between components is non-trivial. Despite the proliferation of hybrid architectures, systematic empirical studies of how each component contributes to model behavior remain scarce. Most architectural papers report aggregate benchmarks but do not decom-pose performance by component. This leaves practitioners without guidance on which components to preserve during compression, which layers to priori-tize during distillation, or how to design more efficient hybrid configurations. 

Contributions.. We present the first comprehensive functional ablation study comparing two fundamentally different hybrid architecture designs: 1. We introduce a reversible ablation framework that can selectively dis-able individual components (attention, SSM, or linear attention) at the group, layer, and positional level, with matched random controls for statistical rigor. 2. We demonstrate through group ablations and perplexity analysis that 

neither component is bypassed : removing the alternative compo-nent (linear attention in Qwen, SSM in Falcon) causes catastrophic perplexity increases of 35,200 × and 53 × respectively, far exceeding the 82 × and 3.2 × degradation from removing attention alone (Section 4.2). 3. We reveal through layer-wise sweeps that component importance follows a positional gradient : early-layer ablations cause 2–5 × more degradation than late-layer ablations, with implications for structured pruning strategies (Section 4.4). 4. We connect behavioral ablation with internal metrics through hidden-state analysis, revealing architecture-dependent relationships: norm change positively predicts ablation impact in the sequential architec-ture (Qwen) but not in the parallel architecture (Falcon), suggesting fundamentally different component integration strategies (Section 4.6). 

2. Related Work 

Hybrid architectures.. The idea of combining attention with recurrent or state-space mechanisms has gained traction as a solution to the efficiency– expressiveness trade-off. Samba [24] demonstrated that a simple hybrid of Mamba and sliding-window attention achieves strong performance with un-limited context, including ablation studies across model sizes up to 1.7B. 3Taipan [12] combines Mamba-2 with selective attention layers that dynami-cally identify tokens requiring long-range interactions. The systematic anal-ysis by Wang et al. [9] trained 72 models at 340M and 1.3B parameters, evaluating six linear attention variants across five hybridization ratios. They found that superior standalone linear models do not necessarily excel in hybrid configurations, and that a linear-to-full ratio between 3:1 and 6:1 achieves Transformer-level recall efficiently. Kimi Linear [10] extended Gated DeltaNet with a finer-grained gating mechanism, demonstrating that their hybrid can outperform full attention with 75% less KV cache. Ren et al. [11] analyzed the limitations of Mamba in copy and chain-of-thought reasoning tasks, showing that while constant-sized Mamba struggles with certain oper-ations, hybridization with attention can overcome these limitations. 

Component analysis in LLMs.. Mechanistic interpretability has studied how individual components contribute to model behavior. Work on circuit dis-covery [13, 14] has mapped specific attention heads to algorithmic functions. However, these studies focus on Transformer architectures exclusively. For hybrid models, Benfeghoul et al. [8] identified that post-training linearized hybrids inadvertently bypass their linear component, relying almost entirely on sliding-window attention. Our work differs by studying natively trained 

hybrids rather than post-hoc conversions. Haller et al. [15] studied what factors matter in linearizing language models, providing background on why gated delta-rule variants behave differently from additive linearized models. 

Ablation methodology.. Component ablation has a long history in neural net-work analysis [16]. Zeroing out activations, as we do for Falcon’s parallel components, is the standard approach for functional ablation. For Qwen’s sequential architecture, we skip entire layers (setting the output to the in-put), which corresponds to identity residual connections. Both approaches measure functional dependence rather than optimal performance without the component, which would require retraining. 

3. Experimental Setup 

3.1. Models 

We study two architecturally distinct hybrid language models in their base (pre-trained, not instruction-tuned) variants: 4Qwen3.5-0.8B. [7] is a 752M-parameter model with 24 decoder layers in a sequential hybrid pattern. It interleaves linear attention layers based on Gated DeltaNet [5] with full softmax attention layers. Of the 24 layers, 18 use linear attention and 6 use full attention (3:1 ratio). Each layer contains only one mechanism type. The model supports a maximum context of 262,144 tokens. 

Falcon-H1-0.5B. [6] is a 521M-parameter model with 36 decoder blocks in a parallel hybrid design. Each block contains both a Mamba-2 SSM path [4] and an attention path that process the input simultaneously. The outputs of both paths are combined within each block, meaning every layer contains both component types. It supports a context length of 16,384 tokens. The choice of sub-1B models is deliberate: it enables exhaustive layer-wise ablation (84 conditions for Falcon, including individual ablation of each com-ponent in each of 36 layers) while keeping the analysis reproducible on a sin-gle GPU. The architectural contrast—sequential vs. parallel hybridization— is the primary variable of interest. As an additional control, we include 

Qwen2.5-0.5B [25], a pure Transformer (490M parameters, 24 layers, soft-max attention only) from the same model family, to determine whether ob-served patterns are hybrid-specific or general properties of deep networks. 

3.2. Ablation Mechanism 

We implement reversible ablation through two strategies adapted to each architecture: 

Sequential skip (Qwen).. For models with distinct layer types, we replace the layer’s output with its input, effectively converting the layer to an identity function within the residual stream: hl+1 = hl instead of hl+1 = hl + fl(hl).

Parallel zeroing (Falcon).. For models with parallel components within each block, we zero out the output contribution of the targeted component via forward hooks: if the original block computes hl+1 = hl + fattn (hl) + fssm (hl),then ablating SSM yields hl+1 = hl + fattn (hl).Both mechanisms are implemented as context-managed forward hooks that are cleanly reversible, preserving model weights for subsequent condi-tions. 53.3. Evaluation Protocol Benchmarks.. We evaluate on five standard benchmarks covering diverse ca-pabilities: MMLU [17] (5-shot, n=2,000; knowledge), GSM8K [18] (8-shot, 

n=50–100; mathematical reasoning), ARC-Challenge [19] (25-shot, n=299; science reasoning), HellaSwag [20] (10-shot, n=2,000; commonsense), and TruthfulQA-MC [21] (0-shot, n=790; factuality). For GSM8K, baseline and group ablation conditions use n=100, while positional ablations use n=50 due to compute constraints; we verified that the reduced sample size does not affect the direction of findings. 

Ablation conditions.. We evaluate four categories totaling 84+ conditions per model: (i) Group ablations : removing all instances of one component type simultaneously; (ii) Layer-wise ablations : removing a single component in a single layer, sweeping across all layers; (iii) Positional ablations : removing all instances of a component within early, middle, or late thirds of the net-work (Falcon); (iv) Matched random controls : removing the same number of randomly selected layers (3 trials per condition). 

Statistical validation.. For all group ablation comparisons, we compute boot-strap confidence intervals (1,000 resamples) on the score drop relative to base-line. A confidence interval excluding zero indicates statistical significance at the 95% level.

5. Discussion 

Both components are essential, but play different roles.. Our most impor-tant finding is that in natively trained hybrid models, neither component is 16 bypassed. This contrasts sharply with the findings of Benfeghoul et al. [8] for post-training linearized models, suggesting that the training procedure (native vs. post-hoc) is the key determinant of whether components achieve genuine specialization. 

The alternative component is the backbone.. The extreme perplexity asym-metry (35,200 × vs. 82 × for Qwen; 53 × vs. 3.2 × for Falcon) reveals that the alternative component—linear attention or SSM—serves as the primary language modeling engine, while attention plays a complementary role. This is consistent with the architectural design intent: Qwen uses 18 linear and only 6 attention layers, while Falcon’s SSM processes every token through state-space dynamics in every block. 

Implications for model compression.. The strong positional gradient in com-ponent importance suggests that pruning strategies for hybrid models should be position-aware. Specifically: (i) early-layer components should be pre-served at higher fidelity; (ii) late-layer components (particularly attention in Falcon) can be more aggressively compressed with minimal performance impact; (iii) distillation efforts should prioritize preserving the linear/SSM pathway over attention, given its backbone role. 

Sequential vs. parallel hybridization.. The two architectures show a qualita-tive difference in component interaction. Qwen’s sequential design creates distinct functional roles for each layer type, enabling clean isolation dur-ing ablation. Falcon’s parallel design results in more symmetric benchmark degradation (both components show similar accuracy drops), but the per-plexity analysis reveals that the SSM is still dominant for language modeling. This suggests that parallel hybridization achieves tighter functional integra-tion between components, while sequential hybridization maintains clearer functional separation. 

Benchmark accuracy vs. perplexity as diagnostic tools.. A methodological insight from our study is that standard benchmarks, while useful for measur-ing task performance, can mask important differences between components. Both component types show similar accuracy drops (Table 1), but perplex-ity reveals a dramatic asymmetry (Table 2). We recommend that future component analysis studies include perplexity as a complementary measure alongside task-specific benchmarks. 17 Hybrid resilience vs. Transformer fragility.. The Transformer baseline com-parison (Table 5) reveals an unexpected finding: hybrid models are sub-stantially more resilient to arbitrary layer removal than pure Transformers of comparable size. When 6 random layers are removed, the Transformer degrades 119 × more than the hybrid in normalized perplexity terms. We at-tribute this to functional redundancy between component types: in a hybrid, disrupting a layer still leaves the alternative component in neighboring lay-ers to partially compensate, creating a distributed backup pathway that pure Transformers lack. This property has practical implications for fault toler-ance in deployed models and suggests that hybrid architectures may degrade more gracefully under hardware errors or aggressive pruning. 

6. Limitations and Future Work 

Scale.. We study sub-1B models to enable exhaustive ablation. Whether the observed specialization patterns persist at 3B, 7B, or larger scales remains an open question. The positional gradient and backbone asymmetry may steepen or flatten with depth, which requires validation with larger models. 

Functional ablation vs. retraining.. Our methodology measures functional dependence at inference time. Zeroing component outputs is not equivalent to training without that component from scratch, and the resulting degra-dation may overestimate true redundancy. This is a standard limitation of ablation studies [16] and should be interpreted as an upper bound on com-ponent importance. 

Cross-architecture comparability.. The ablation mechanisms are necessarily architecture-specific: for Qwen, disabling a layer removes both the token mixer and its associated MLP, while for Falcon, zeroing a parallel compo-nent preserves the MLP and the other component. This means the interven-tions are not directly comparable across architectures. We address this by reporting within-architecture contrasts (attention vs. alternative component for each model) rather than cross-architecture comparisons, and by includ-ing matched random controls to calibrate the expected degradation for each architecture independently. 

Benchmark coverage.. Standard benchmarks use relatively short prompts that may not fully expose the benefits of the linear/SSM pathway for long-context processing. Our optional context-length stress test (up to 1,024 18 tokens) shows stable degradation patterns, but testing at 4K–128K tokens would be valuable to characterize long-context component reliance. 

Future directions.. Natural extensions include: (a) scaling validation at 3B– 7B with a reduced set of critical ablation conditions; (b) studying how compo-nent specialization evolves during training by ablating at intermediate check-points; (c) using the ablation framework to guide hybrid architecture search, e.g., optimizing the linear-to-attention ratio; and (d) applying the methodol-ogy to other hybrid families such as RWKV [22], Griffin [23], or Samba [24].

7. Conclusion 

We have presented a comprehensive functional ablation framework for hybrid language models, applied to two architecturally contrasting sub-1B hybrid models—Qwen3.5-0.8B (sequential hybrid with Gated DeltaNet + softmax attention) and Falcon-H1-0.5B (parallel hybrid with Mamba-2 + attention)—with a pure Transformer control (Qwen2.5-0.5B). Our experi-ments, spanning 84+ ablation conditions per model, five benchmarks, per-plexity analysis, and hidden-state metrics, establish four findings with prac-tical implications: First, both component types are genuinely utilized in natively trained hybrid models—neither is bypassed, contrasting with recent observations in post-training linearized models. Second, the alternative component (linear attention or SSM) serves as the primary language modeling backbone, with attention providing complementary refinement, as evidenced by the perplex-ity degradation ratio between the two removal conditions (Qwen: 35,200 × vs. 82 ×; Falcon: 53 × vs. 3.2 ×). Third, component importance follows a strong early-layer gradient, with the first few layers being disproportionately criti-cal for model function.
